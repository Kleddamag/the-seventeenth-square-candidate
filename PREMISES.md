# The fifteen mathematical premises

Status: candidate proof, not an established theorem. The status is stated once, in `README.md` ("Status"), and in full in `STATEMENT_STATUS.md`.

A premise here is a lemma that the proof text uses. For each one the edition carries the paragraphs of the proof text and the files listed in the table below; for some premises these files are tables and programs only, not a separate written proof. None has been verified by anyone outside the project.

The table is made from `PKT/OBLIGATIONS.json` (the claims), `PKT/revision-v7/LABELS.tsv` (the files of record) and the proof text (the paragraphs); `PKT/` stands for `research/finishing-threshold-goal-20261007/current-author-20261012t/fifth-order-20261010/public-edition-round2-20261011/`. `READING_ORDER.md`, part 3, gives for each premise the whole claim, every file of record, the review reports and what it depends on.

- **What it says:** the first sentence of the claim, as written in `OBLIGATIONS.json`. It is not the whole claim.
- **Kind:** read from the files, by one rule. "Analytic argument": a text (`.md`) is among the files of record of the premise. "Computation with certificates": a data file or a program is among its files of record, or its evidence files in `OBLIGATIONS.json` include a program, a log, a certificate or a receipt. "Both": both hold. The rule looks at kinds of files; it does not judge the argument.
- **Where the argument is:** the paragraphs of `PROOF.md` that carry the label of the premise, and its files of record (the last two parts of each path; full paths in `READING_ORDER.md`).
